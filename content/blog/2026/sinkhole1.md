Update - [vendor1] called me and wanted to reschedule a week later, so we
went down that evening with my wall cavity camera and inspected the
corrugated metal pipe under the roadway (saving the SID $350).  A few pictures
are included below.  From that inspection, I concluded that the full 90' of
storm drain pipe including the portion underneath the roadway would need to be
replaced because there are significant holes in the bottom portion of the pipe
at both inlets, a sinkhole under a good portion of the pipe beyond the roadway
and the outfall is crushed.  I know the engineer that assesses the condition of
sewers and storm drains for the city of Omaha and I sent him the photos; he was
curious if this might be a candidate for lining vs. replacement.  Based on the
photos, he also concluded that the entire storm drain would need to be replaced
including the portion under the roadway.  He recommended RCP (reinforced
concrete pipe) because of how shallow the storm drain is.  The current CMP pipe
has been crushed into an ellipse (instead of a circle) under the roadway due to
how shallow it is and roadway/vehicular loading.  He estimated the cost to be
$50k-$80k and provided the name of 3 KTRs that do similar scale work for the
City of Omaha.  I've reached out to these KTRs today to start the process of
getting quotes with the goal of receiving quotes prior to the 8 September
meeting.

On the good news front, I received the additional ammonia test results from
[vendor2] that confirms that the WWTF is treating ammonia.  Influent ammonia
tested at 104 mg/L; whereas, ammonia directly prior to chlorination (towards
the end of the process) measured at 0.36 mg/L.  I tested before chlorination
because I wanted to rule out the possible ammonia interaction with chlorine.
These results (successfully treating ammonia) are in line with the last two
quarterly testing results!  These results make it viable to continue to invest
in the SID's WWTF.  I think that even if the SID is going to replace the WWTF
with a cluster septic system, the SID should probably complete the currently
needed repairs to satisfy the NDWEE and buy some time.
