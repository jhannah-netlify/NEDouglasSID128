**Bad news first:** The Q2 effluent sample exceeded the CBOD (Carbonaceous
Biological Oxygen Demand) limits by a little and exceeded E coli limits by
about 700%.  The chlorination/dechlorination system is not as effective at
treating E coli when the clarifier system is down because waste solids are
bulking (floating) into the chlorine contact chamber instead of being
recirculated into the aeration basin.  I expect that we will receive a Letter
of Noncompliance from the NDWEE that will require action.

**Good news 1:** The same quarterly sample that exceeded the E coli limits was
under the ammonia limits (FOR THE FIRST TIME!!!).  Not sure if this is a fluke
or the real deal.  With the new controller that the SID installed for the
blowers, I am able to fine tune the amount of air introduced to the aeration
basin and with the new optical dissolved oxygen meter, I can accurately measure
dissolved oxygen.  These two upgrades have allowed me to maintain 2.0 - 3.0
mg/L dissolved oxygen in the aeration basin, which is the sweet spot for
treating ammonia.  I plan to take the Q3 sample next week to verify.

**Good news 2:** We received another quote for repair of the clarifier from a local
(North Omaha) mechanical contractor, see attached [any homeowner can request
this attachment from any Board Member, or our Lawyer].  The quote is $65k, but
I estimate the cost would be closer to $70k by the time the clarifier is pumped
out and power washed to prep for the work to be completed.  This is a
significantly better price than the $115k price based on other quotes and it
involves the Contractor both purchasing and installing the equipment instead of
the SID purchasing the materials and hiring a Contractor to install it.  Also,
the lead time is 3 week for the materials with this quote vs. 16-18 weeks with
the previous supplier.  One item of particular concern is that the proposed
wall bearings that hold the shafts are not specifically for wastewater
applications, but appears to be the best option that the Contractor could find.
I wouldn't be surprised if the Letter of Noncompliance from the NDWEE pushes
the SID in this direction.

On the cluster septic system, we are still waiting to see if the SID could
issue bonds to fund it and what those terms would be.  Optimally, we could
utilize State Revolving Funds (SRF) as a low-interest 20 or 30 year loan, but
SRF funds won't be available again until 2030 at the earliest.
